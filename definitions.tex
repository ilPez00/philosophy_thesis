\chapter*{Definitions}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Definitions}

\noindent This thesis uses a small vocabulary of Greek, Sanskrit, and
reconstructed Indo-European terms in precise senses. They are gathered here so the
argument can proceed without interrupting itself to gloss them.

\begin{description}

\item[\textsc{Ayu}] (Sanskrit \emph{āyu}; Proto-Indo-European \emph{*h\textsubscript{2}óyu-},
``vital force, allotted life-span, eternity''; cf.\ Latin \emph{aevum}, English
\emph{aye}, \emph{ever}, and \emph{aeon}). The living force one is given to spend:
not life as a biological fact but life as a finite allotment of vitality, a fund
of action. The term carries at once \emph{life} and \emph{eternity} --- the
intuition that a life spent in coherence reaches, through what it leaves behind,
toward the undying. The thesis takes \physis\ to be the governor of how this
\textsc{ayu} is rightly spent.

\item[\textsc{Theorein}] (Greek \emph{θεωρεῖν}, ``to behold, to contemplate''; the
root of \emph{theory}, \emph{theoria}). Not detached spectating but the seeing
that completes knowing --- the beholding of the order of a thing. It names the
highest segment of the divided line (Book VI), where the practitioner no longer
follows rules but \emph{sees} what nature requires. The review of one's own
coherence graph is a \emph{theorein}: a contemplation of the shape of one's own
action.

\item[\textsc{Aner -- nar -- h\textsubscript{2}ner -- vir}] the Indo-European
lineage of ``man as bearer of vital and heroic force.'' Greek \emph{anēr}
(\emph{ἀνήρ}, ``man, hero,'' whence \emph{andreia}, courage); Sanskrit \emph{nár-,
nr̥} (man, vital power); Proto-Indo-European \emph{*h\textsubscript{2}nér-} (the
sovereign masculine force, the life-power that \emph{acts}); Latin \emph{vir}
(man, whence \emph{virtus}, virtue). The thread names not a sex but a posture: the
\emph{agathos}, the excellent one whose nature is to act well and to spend his
\textsc{ayu} in form-seeking deed. The practitioner is the heir of this lineage ---
\emph{vir} become \emph{virtus}, vital force become excellence.

\item[\textsc{Agathos} / \textsc{Kleos}] \emph{agathos} (Greek, ``good, noble,
excellent''): the one whose action is excellent, in the Homeric and aristocratic
sense before the moralised one. \emph{kleos} (Greek, ``glory, that which is
heard''): the undying fame the hero wins when his deed is remembered and re-sung.
The thesis argues (Book X) that the UberWiki is the modern instrument of
\emph{kleos} --- immortal glory among mortals --- because the coherent deed
persists as a node others enact.

\item[\physis\ / \praxis\ / \techne] nature (the active principle of growth and
self-organisation) / action (done for its own sake, expressing the agent's
character) / craft-knowledge (the art of making). The thesis: \praxis\ is the
tool of \physis, and \techne\ is just when it serves them, unjust when it usurps.

\item[\textsc{Entelecheia}] (Aristotle) ``being-at-work-staying-itself'': the
state in which a thing has its end within it and is actively realising it. The
form a being is already striving to become.

\item[\textsc{Coherence} ($+1$ / $0$ / $-1$)] nature's verdict on an action:
a real registered effect ($+1$), no effect ($0$), or self-violation ($-1$). Not a
moral score but a measured relation between declared intent and registered effect.

\item[\textsc{Holarchy} / \textsc{UberWiki}] the single shared graph of
coherence-weighted action into which every practitioner deposits what worked; the
commons of Book X, the \emph{anamnesis} of the philosophical machines.

\item[\emph{Das Man}] (Heidegger) the anonymous ``they'' --- the impersonal public
standard (``what one does'') that governs inauthentically because no one authors
it. The thesis treats the recommendation algorithm as \emph{das Man}
operationalised, and the UberWiki as its re-authorisation.

\item[\textsc{Dream}] the engine's background loop that replays inert and failing
nodes and proposes corrective variations --- nature's faculty of variation made
algorithmic.

\end{description}
